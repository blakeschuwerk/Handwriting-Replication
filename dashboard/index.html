Fine-tune on your handwriting

      
Start small (fewer epochs) and watch the live sample. Stop when it looks like your hand — more isn't always better (it can overfit).

      


      

        What is this page actually doing? (read this first)
        

          
There's a program on this Mac (FW-GAN) that already knows how to draw generic
          cursive handwriting — it learned that from thousands of other people's handwriting before you
          ever used this app. Fine-tuning means showing it your 1,975 words over and over, each time
          nudging its internal settings a tiny bit toward matching your strokes, until its handwriting
          starts looking like yours instead of a stranger's.

          
Every field below controls how that nudging happens:

          
    
            
  • Epoch = one full pass through all 1,975 of your words. More epochs = it sees your
            handwriting more times, which usually helps — up to a point where it starts memorizing your
            exact words instead of learning your general style (called overfitting).
    • 
            
  • Step = one nudge, using one small batch of words at a time (not all 1,975 at once —
            too much to hold in memory). One epoch = many steps.
    • 
            
  • Learning rate = how big each nudge is. Too big and it can overshoot and produce
            garbage; too small and it barely moves in any reasonable amount of time.
    • 
            
  • Loss (in the stats below, once running) = a score for "how wrong is it right now" —
            you want to see this trend down, not need to understand the exact number.
    • 
          

          
You don't have to get any of this exactly right. The XY pad below turns the two settings that
          matter most into one drag — and you can always watch the live sample and stop early if it looks
          wrong.

Write on your own paper

      
Photograph a page of ruled paper, type what you want, and it gets written onto that page in your handwriting — following the paper's real lines, perspective and lighting. Tab indents, Enter starts a new line, and long lines wrap at the margin.
